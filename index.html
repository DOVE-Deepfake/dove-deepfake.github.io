DOVE is a research prototype designed specifically to detect deepfakes in video on demand. By analysing buffered segments ahead of playback, it can look into the future of the video, spot potentially manipulated parts, and warn viewers before they are exposed to them.

DOVE Research Prototype v0.1

01 / THE IDEA

Detect deepfakes.
Locate them in time.

DOVEDeepfake On-demand Video Evaluator

DOVE is a research prototype that extends MPEG-DASH video-on-demand playback with analysis on the viewer’s device. It extracts frames from buffered video segments and analyses the faces they contain, giving each segment a real-or-deepfake label through deepfake classification.

Because each segment corresponds to a known interval in the video, its classification also tells us where potential manipulation occurs. This enables temporal localization: an approximate indication of the manipulated parts within the full video, since their exact start and end may fall inside a segment.

DOVE puts deepfake analysis in the viewer’s hands, without requiring cooperation from the streaming platform. Analysing each segment can help reveal even short manipulations within long videos, while detections in early segments may provide enough evidence to suggest that the entire video is manipulated.

03 / ROOTED IN RESEARCH

An academic project.
With potential beyond the lab.

DOVE is developed by Christian Coduri as part of his PhD research at Politecnico di Torino, supervised by Prof. Alessio Sacco and Prof. Guido Marchetto. It explores how deepfake detection can become part of the video on-demand streaming experience.

Research roadmap.

What DOVE supports today, what we are developing, and what we plan to investigate next.

Implemented

  • MPEG-DASH support

    Analyze downloaded video segments alongside buffered playback in MPEG-DASH streams.

  • Spatial deepfake detection

    Two frame-extraction strategies support classification: one selects a single frame per segment; the other adapts frame sampling to segment duration.

  • Segment-level temporal localization

    Mapping detections to segment time intervals gives an approximate location of manipulated content within the full video.

In progress

  • Spatiotemporal analysis

    Combine spatial evidence with relationships across frames to detect manipulations.

  • Timeliness measurement

    Measure warning lead time and how often analysis finishes before playback, compared with detection as content is displayed.

  • Evaluation and comparisons

    Compare detectors and segment configurations across devices, measuring false alarms, missed manipulations, processing cost, energy use and playback impact.

Future directions

Low-quality segment classification

Investigate detection robustness under compression and at low video resolutions.

Audio-segment analysis

Extend segment-level inspection to audio, alongside video analysis.

Beyond deepfakes

Apply buffered-segment analysis to other classification tasks, including harmful-content detection and parental controls.
